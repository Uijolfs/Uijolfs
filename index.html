Between the world
and its models.

I am interested in how physical reasoning and mathematical models help us understand complex systems—what they reveal, what they leave out, and where they stop being useful.

What draws me to a problem also lies beyond the model itself: why it matters, and who might benefit from solving it. Clinical questions bring these interests together, connecting the search for understanding with the possibility of improving people's lives.

About

Xian Chen

I am an undergraduate double majoring in Physics and Electrical & Computer Engineering at Worcester Polytechnic Institute. Before transferring to WPI, I spent two years studying physics at Lanzhou University.

My current research focuses on computational cardiac electrophysiology. This site also collects my notes and experiments in physics, neuroscience, nonlinear dynamics, and mathematical methods for physics.

Research

Questions I'm working on.

Connecting electrical activity, mathematical models, and clinical problems.

01 / MODELCardiac modeling & digital twins+

How can a computational model capture the essential behavior of cardiac electrical propagation?

My work includes cellular automata models and simulations linking cardiac activation to ECG signals.

02 / LOCATEECG-based localization+

What can a small set of ECG measurements tell us about the location of an electrical source?

I study computational approaches to pace mapping, using the relationship between spatial position and ECG features.

03 / NAVIGATEComputational catheter navigation+

How can anatomical information and electrical measurements help guide the next step?

I explore geometry-aware approaches to navigation on cardiac surfaces.

Descriptions here are an overview. Code and publications will be linked as they become available for public release.
